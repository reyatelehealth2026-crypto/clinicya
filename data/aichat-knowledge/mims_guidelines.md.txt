

# Comprehensive Clinical Practice Guidelines Collection for Thailand: RAG-Ready Multidisciplinary Dataset

## 1. Executive Summary & Strategic Framework

### 1.1 Primary Objective

#### 1.1.1 Build comprehensive RAG dataset from Thailand-optimized CPG sources

The fundamental purpose of this initiative is to construct a **comprehensive, Thailand-optimized Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) dataset** derived from authoritative Clinical Practice Guidelines (CPG) sources. This endeavor addresses the critical need for evidence-based, structured medical knowledge systems that can power next-generation clinical decision support tools specifically tailored to the Thai healthcare context. Unlike generic international guideline compilations, this project prioritizes **local adaptation**—accounting for Thailand's distinctive disease epidemiology (including tropical infectious diseases with persistent burden alongside rising non-communicable diseases), medication availability within the National List of Essential Medicines and universal health coverage schemes, healthcare infrastructure variations between Bangkok-centric tertiary centers and rural primary care facilities, and cultural considerations affecting patient-provider communication and treatment adherence.

The dataset construction follows a **three-phase methodology**: systematic discovery and retrieval from multiple validated sources; sophisticated content structuring with hierarchical decomposition and rich metadata annotation; and optimized vector database integration enabling semantic search and natural language query response. This architecture ensures that the resulting RAG system retrieves **precisely relevant, evidence-grounded recommendations** with full traceability to source documents, version information, and evidence grades—essential requirements for clinical accountability and regulatory compliance in healthcare AI applications.

#### 1.1.2 Prioritize MIMS Thailand as flagship platform with diagnostic/treatment algorithms

**MIMS Thailand emerges as the flagship platform** for this initiative based on its established position as the most comprehensive collection of clinical practice guidelines in the region, its sophisticated integration of diagnostic algorithms with medication reference data, and its trusted reputation among Thai healthcare professionals. The platform's **disease algorithm format**—presenting stepwise diagnostic and therapeutic pathways as visually structured decision trees—provides immediate clinical utility while facilitating automated extraction for RAG construction. MIMS Thailand's **50+ years of operation across 17 Asia-Pacific countries**  demonstrates sustained investment in content quality and technological infrastructure, with its Thailand-specific iteration reflecting deep localization for local prescribing patterns, formulary constraints, and disease priorities.

The prioritization of MIMS is strategically grounded in **practical clinical adoption**: Thai physicians already rely on this platform for point-of-care decision support, ensuring smoother integration of RAG-enhanced applications into existing workflows. The platform's **mobile application with 40,000+ active users**  and **2 million+ healthcare professional reach across Asia**  indicates successful penetration of the target market. Unlike academic guideline repositories that may lag in update frequency or medication integration, MIMS Thailand combines **current evidence synthesis with immediate prescribing practicality**—a combination essential for real-world clinical impact.

#### 1.1.3 Ensure multidisciplinary coverage across 15 medical specialties

The scope encompasses **complete specialty coverage** spanning: **Cardiology, Dermatology, Endocrinology, Gastroenterology, Hepatology, Infectious Diseases, Neurology, Obstetrics & Gynaecology, Oncology, Ophthalmology, Paediatrics, Psychiatry, Respirology, Rheumatology, and Urology**. This taxonomy reflects both traditional medical departmental structures and Thailand's specific disease burden priorities, ensuring that the RAG dataset can support clinical decision-making across the full spectrum of patient presentations—from common ambulatory complaints to complex, multi-system diseases requiring coordinated specialist care.

The multidisciplinary architecture explicitly accommodates **natural specialty overlaps** (such as Endocrinology-Cardiology intersection in metabolic syndrome management, or Infectious Diseases-Hepatology integration in viral hepatitis care) through cross-referencing mechanisms rather than rigid categorical separation. This design supports **holistic patient care** in an era of increasing multimorbidity, where single-disease guidelines may provide inadequate guidance for complex, real-world patients.

### 1.2 Core Value Proposition

#### 1.2.1 High-quality patient care guidance through evidence-based algorithms

The first pillar ensures that every retrieved recommendation is **grounded in systematic evidence review** and **graded according to established hierarchies of evidence**. MIMS Thailand exemplifies this commitment through its **specialty-specific Honorary Editorial Advisory Boards** that review and validate algorithm content—such as the MIMS Cardiology Honorary Editorial Advisory Board's oversight of heart failure and hypertension protocols , or the MIMS Dermatology Honorary Editorial Advisory Board's review of atopic dermatitis management pathways . These governance structures ensure that algorithms reflect **current scientific consensus** rather than opinion-based approaches, with explicit "Last updated" timestamps enabling currency assessment.

The **evidence-based algorithm format** addresses well-documented variations in clinical practice that compromise patient outcomes. By encoding stepwise pathways—from presenting complaint through differential diagnosis, confirmatory testing, severity stratification, treatment selection, and monitoring protocols—the dataset provides **decision support that reduces cognitive load** while maintaining appropriate flexibility for individual patient circumstances. This standardization proves particularly valuable in Thailand's heterogeneous healthcare landscape, where provider training and resource availability vary substantially between tertiary academic centers and provincial hospitals.

#### 1.2.2 Practitioner-ready compilation with structured diagnostic pathways

The second pillar addresses the **implementation gap** that has historically limited guideline impact. Traditional CPG dissemination through journal publication or society website posting suffers from **poor discoverability, format inconsistency, and absence of workflow integration**. MIMS Thailand transcends these limitations through its **standardized tripartite content structure**: Disease Algorithm (visual flowcharts with branching decision logic), Disease Guidelines (narrative evidence-based recommendations with supporting rationale), and Medication Reference (integrated prescribing information with Thai-specific availability and cost data) .

This **practitioner-ready design** enables rapid information retrieval during time-pressured clinical encounters. The hierarchical organization—**Overview → History and Physical Examination → Diagnosis/Diagnostic Criteria → Laboratory Tests and Ancillaries → Imaging → Differential Diagnosis → Evaluation Principles of Therapy → Pharmacological therapy → Nonpharmacological interventions → Surgery → Monitoring**—mirrors clinical reasoning patterns while supporting both **quick reference** (for experienced practitioners) and **comprehensive review** (for complex cases or educational purposes). The integration of medication reference data eliminates friction between "what to prescribe" and "how to prescribe it"—a critical efficiency gain in busy practice settings.

#### 1.2.3 Scalable vector database integration for AI-powered clinical decision support

The third pillar positions the dataset for **next-generation healthcare AI applications**. The transformation of structured CPG content into **semantically rich vector embeddings** with preserved metadata enables novel interaction modalities: **natural language querying** ("What is the recommended antihypertensive regimen for a diabetic patient with albuminuria?"), **similarity-based retrieval** of analogous case management approaches, and **contextual augmentation** of large language model outputs with authoritative medical knowledge. This represents a paradigm shift from **passive guideline publication** to **active, context-aware clinical intelligence**.

The technical architecture employs **medical domain-specific embedding models** (PubMedBERT, BioBERT) with **512-token chunking and 128-token overlap**, **hybrid dense-sparse indexing** for precise terminology matching, and **hierarchical metadata tagging** (specialty → disease → algorithm step → recommendation with evidence grade, version, and source URL). This infrastructure supports **sub-second retrieval latency** at scale, with explicit **provenance preservation** that maintains clinical accountability and enables continuous quality improvement through outcome tracking.

## 2. MIMS Thailand: Primary CPG Platform Architecture

### 2.1 Platform Capabilities

#### 2.1.1 Disease-specific clinical practice guidelines with treatment algorithms

MIMS Thailand's **disease algorithm module** represents its most distinctive capability for RAG applications. These algorithms present **stepwise diagnostic and therapeutic pathways** as visual flowcharts with explicit decision nodes, enabling practitioners to navigate from presenting complaint through definitive management with systematic precision. The **Chronic Coronary Syndromes algorithm**, for instance, incorporates the **Canadian Cardiovascular Society Angina Classification (Classes I-IV)** and **2024 European Society of Cardiology chronic coronary syndrome categories**, progressing through risk stratification, diagnostic confirmation, and evidence-based pharmacological management with specific medication class recommendations .

The algorithmic structure encodes **clinical reasoning logic** that translates readily into machine-actionable knowledge graphs: nodes representing clinical states (e.g., "stable angina with CCS Class II symptoms"), edges representing decision criteria (e.g., "stress imaging positive for ischemia"), and terminal actions (e.g., "initiate high-intensity statin plus aspirin, refer for coronary angiography"). This **explicit procedural knowledge** contrasts with narrative guidelines that require substantial interpretation for implementation, reducing variability in clinical application and supporting quality standardization.

The **temporal currency** of algorithm content is ensured through systematic review cycles with **explicit update timestamps**—such as the **26 January 2026 update for Hypertension** , **11 December 2025 for Chronic Coronary Syndromes** , and **16 March 2026 for Dyspepsia** —enabling automated surveillance for content refresh and version control in RAG deployments.

#### 2.1.3 Integrated medication reference with prescribing information

The **medication reference integration** distinguishes MIMS Thailand from standalone guideline repositories by embedding **immediately actionable prescribing guidance** within treatment pathways. For cardiovascular conditions, this includes organized presentation of **ACE Inhibitors, Angiotensin II Antagonists, Antiplatelet Agents, Beta-Blockers, Calcium Antagonists, Cardiac Drugs, and Nitrate formulations** with Thai-specific product availability, dosing, and cost data . For gastroenterology, **Antacids, H2-Receptor Antagonists, Proton Pump Inhibitors, and specialized agents like Cholagogues and Hepatic Protectors** are similarly integrated .

This **"closed-loop" decision support** enables practitioners to progress from diagnostic reasoning to therapeutic implementation without platform switching—a critical efficiency gain that addresses the **fragmentation pain point** in clinical workflow. The medication data encompasses: **indications and contraindications** with Thai regulatory status; **dosing regimens** with renal/hepatic adjustment and special population considerations; **adverse effect profiles** with monitoring requirements; **drug-drug interactions** relevant to common Thai co-prescribing patterns; **pregnancy/lactation safety** with Thai FDA categorizations; and **cost information** relevant to Thailand's three universal health coverage schemes (Universal Coverage Scheme, Social Security Scheme, Civil Servant Medical Benefit Scheme).

### 2.2 Specialty Coverage Matrix

#### 2.2.1 Cardiovascular: Heart Failure, Hypertension, Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy, Hypertensive Crisis

The **cardiovascular portfolio** addresses Thailand's **leading cause of mortality** with substantial depth and currency. The **Heart Failure - Chronic** algorithm (updated 27 February 2026)  provides comprehensive staging and management guidance, distinguishing **HFrEF, HFmrEF, and HFpEF phenotypes** with phenotype-specific therapeutic pathways. The content integrates **2020 Heart Failure Society of Singapore clinical practice guidelines**  with Thai adaptation for medication availability, emphasizing **four foundational drug classes** (ARNI/ACE inhibitors, evidence-based beta-blockers, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, and SGLT2 inhibitors) with explicit up-titration targets to "maximum recommended dose or the highest dose that the patient can tolerate" .

The **Hypertension** algorithm (updated 26 January 2026)  aligns with **2024 Thai Hypertension Society guidelines** , maintaining **office blood pressure diagnostic threshold of 140/90 mmHg** while advocating for **out-of-office measurement confirmation** through home blood pressure monitoring or ambulatory blood pressure monitoring. Treatment targets are **age-stratified**: 120-130/70-79 mmHg for patients aged 18-65 years, and 130-139/70-79 mmHg for patients over 65 years . The algorithm emphasizes **dual antihypertensive initiation** for most patients, with preference for **single-pill combinations** to improve adherence, and explicit **resistant hypertension evaluation protocols** with secondary cause assessment and specialist referral triggers.

The **Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy** and **Hypertensive Crisis** algorithms address **high-stakes, less common presentations** where appropriate management requires specific expertise. These entries include **prominent red-flag identification**, **immediate stabilization protocols**, and **clear criteria for emergent versus urgent versus routine specialist referral**—supporting appropriate triage and resource utilization across Thailand's tiered healthcare system.

The **Contact Dermatitis** algorithm distinguishes **irritant versus allergic mechanisms** with **patch testing indications and interpretation**, **occupational dermatology considerations** relevant to Thailand's agricultural and industrial workforce, and **avoidance counseling** with practical implementation guidance. ### 2.3 Content Structure per Disease Entry

#### 2.3.1 Disease Algorithm: Stepwise diagnostic and therapeutic pathways

The **Disease Algorithm** component presents **visual, interactive flowcharts** that encode clinical decision logic in immediately comprehensible formats. These algorithms typically follow a **standardized progression**:

| Stage | Content Elements | Example: Asthma Management  |
|-------|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Initial Assessment** | Presenting symptoms, risk factors, baseline measurements | Daytime symptoms, activity limitation, nocturnal symptoms, reliever use frequency |
| **Control Evaluation** | Structured scoring systems, severity stratification | Controlled/partly controlled/uncontrolled classification across four domains |
| **Diagnostic Confirmation** | Objective testing, differential diagnosis exclusion | Spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility, alternative diagnosis evaluation |
| **Treatment Initiation** | First-line therapy selection by severity/control level | Step 2: Low-dose ICS-formoterol maintenance and reliever |
| **Monitoring & Adjustment** | Response assessment, step-up/step-down criteria | Review every 3-6 months, escalate if uncontrolled, de-escalate if well-controlled ≥3 months |
| **Specialist Referral** | Red flags, refractory disease, complex comorbidity | Severe asthma, diagnostic uncertainty, frequent exacerbations despite Step 4 |

This **explicit procedural structure** enables both **human clinical application** and **machine-actionable extraction** for RAG dataset construction, with natural decision boundaries supporting coherent chunking.

#### 2.3.2 Disease Guidelines: Evidence-based management recommendations

The RCPT maintains **systematic processes for guideline version control and scheduled review**, ensuring that practitioners can **identify current guidance and understand recommendation evolution** . The **2025 Guideline Development Manual** establishes standardized approaches for **literature search, evidence synthesis, recommendation formulation, and external review** .

The **evidence grading system**—typically employing **modified GRADE methodology** or **Class I/II/III with Level A/B/C classification**—enables transparent assessment of recommendation strength:

| Classification | Definition | Clinical Implication |
|--------------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Class I** | Evidence and/or general agreement that treatment is beneficial, useful, effective | Should be performed/administered |
| **Class IIa** | Conflicting evidence, but weight of evidence favors benefit | Reasonable to perform |
| **Class IIb** | Conflicting evidence, usefulness/efficacy less well established | May be considered |
| **Class III** | Evidence and/or general agreement that treatment is not useful/effective and may be harmful | Should not be performed |
| **Level A** | Multiple randomized clinical trials or meta-analyses | High certainty |
| **Level B** | Single randomized trial or non-randomized studies | Moderate certainty |
| **Level C** | Expert opinion, case studies, standard of care | Limited certainty |

This **granular grading system** enables **RAG retrieval prioritization** by evidence strength and **appropriate confidence calibration** in clinical decision support applications.

## 6. RAG Dataset Construction Methodology

### 6.1 Data Acquisition Pipeline

#### 6.1.1 Automated scraping: MIMS Thailand disease pages with algorithm extraction

The **automated scraping pipeline** for MIMS Thailand targets the **structured disease algorithm pages** with **predictable URL patterns** (`https://www.mims.com/thailand/disease/{condition}/{section}`) . Technical requirements include:

| Component | Specification | Rationale |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Browser automation** | Selenium/Playwright with JavaScript execution | Dynamic content rendering for algorithm visualizations |
| **Respectful rate limiting** | ≤1 request/second with exponential backoff | Service continuity and terms of service compliance |
| **Structured extraction** | XPath/CSS selectors for algorithm steps, decision nodes, medication tables | Preservation of hierarchical clinical logic |
| **Error handling** | Fallback parsing for page structure variations; manual review queue for extraction failures | Dataset completeness assurance |
| **Version detection** | Timestamp and editorial board metadata capture | Currency tracking and update triggering |

The **algorithm-specific extraction** targets: **visual flowchart elements** (decision diamonds, action rectangles, terminal nodes); **branching condition text** with threshold values and clinical criteria; **sequential step numbering** preserving procedural order; and **cross-reference links** to medication and guideline sections.

#### 6.1.2 PDF processing: RCPT and specialty society guideline documents

**PDF processing** addresses **heterogeneous document formats** with **adaptive pipeline architecture**:

| Document Type | Processing Approach | Output Structure |
|-------------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Born-digital PDFs** | Direct text extraction with layout preservation; heading hierarchy identification | Structured sections with metadata (title, version, authors, date) |
| **Scanned documents** | OCR with Thai-English language detection; figure and table segmentation | Searchable text with confidence scoring; manual verification queue for low-confidence regions |
| **Mixed-format documents** | Hybrid approach with zone-based processing; human-in-the-loop for complex layouts | Best-effort extraction with explicit uncertainty flags |

**Critical preservation targets**: **evidence grading annotations** (Class I/II/III, Level A/B/C); **algorithm flowcharts** with vector graphic extraction or alternative text generation; **medication dosing tables** with structured row-column relationships; and **reference citations** with PubMed ID resolution where possible.

#### 6.1.3 API integration: PubMed, Cochrane for evidence linkage

**API integration** enables **provenance enrichment and living guideline functionality**:

- **PubMed E-utilities**: Citation resolution from guideline reference lists; abstract and metadata retrieval for supporting studies; MeSH term extraction for semantic enrichment
- **Cochrane Library**: Systematic review identification for guideline evidence base; intervention effect size data for outcome prediction
- **CrossRef**: DOI resolution and forward citation tracking for evidence update detection

This integration supports **"evidence-aware" RAG retrieval** that surfaces both **the Thai guideline recommendation** and its **supporting primary research** when users query about specific interventions.

### 6.2 Content Structuring & Chunking

#### 6.2.1 Hierarchical decomposition: Specialty → Disease → Algorithm Step → Recommendation

The **four-level hierarchical decomposition** enables **retrieval granularity matching query specificity**:

| Level | Typical Content Scope | Chunk Size | Retrieval Application |
|-------|----------------------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Specialty** | Disease prevalence, health system organization, screening programs | 1024-2048 tokens | Broad context queries; health policy questions; continuing medical education |
| **Disease** | Epidemiology, pathophysiology, classification systems, overview algorithms | 512-1024 tokens | Diagnostic framework questions; patient education material generation |
| **Algorithm Step** | Single decision node with conditions, actions, alternatives; complete pathway segment | 256-512 tokens | Specific clinical scenario queries; point-of-care decision support |
| **Recommendation** | Evidence-graded guidance statement with rationale, special populations, implementation notes | 128-256 tokens | Precise therapeutic questions; drug interaction checking; medicolegal documentation |

**Boundary preservation strategies**: **algorithm step integrity** (avoid splitting conditional logic across chunks); **medication information cohesion** (dosing, monitoring, interactions in proximate chunks); and **evidence grade-context association** (recommendation statement with its supporting rationale and citations).

#### 6.2.2 Metadata tagging: Version, evidence grade, source URL, publication date

**Comprehensive metadata schema** for each chunk:

| Metadata Category | Specific Fields | Clinical Utility |
|-------------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Provenance** | Source platform (MIMS Thailand, RCPT, specific society); document URL; document version; chunk creation timestamp | Citation verification; update detection; accountability documentation |
| **Clinical Context** | Specialty; disease/condition; ICD-10 codes; SNOMED CT concepts; patient population tags (adult, pediatric, pregnant, elderly) | Query filtering; relevance ranking; contraindication checking |
| **Evidence Quality** | Evidence class (I/II/III); evidence level (A/B/C); guideline section type (algorithm, narrative, medication) | Confidence calibration; quality filtering; shared decision-making support |
| **International Correlation** | Source international guideline (ACC/AHA, ESC, ADA, etc.); adaptation notes; divergence rationale | Cross-reference retrieval; guideline harmonization assessment |

#### 6.2.3 Cross-reference mapping: International guideline correlations

**Bidirectional cross-reference mapping** documents relationships between **Thai guidelines and international sources**: explicit citation links (Thai guideline references international source); semantic similarity (algorithm step corresponds to international recommendation); and adaptation annotations (Thai modification with documented rationale). This mapping enables **retrieval augmentation** that presents **both local recommendation and international context** when users query about guideline provenance or potential practice variation.

### 6.3 Vector Database Optimization

#### 6.3.1 Embedding model selection: Medical domain-specific (PubMedBERT, BioBERT)

| Model | Training Corpus | Strengths for Thai CPG | Deployment Considerations |
|-------|---------------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| **PubMedBERT**  | 14 million PubMed abstracts | Superior biomedical terminology capture; strong baseline for medical QA | English-only; requires Thai content translation or parallel processing |
| **BioBERT**  | PubMed abstracts + PMC full-text + clinical notes (MIMIC-III) | Rich clinical concept relationships; procedure and medication understanding | Similar English limitation; v1.1 with multi-domain pretraining recommended |
| **mBERT/XLM-R** | Multilingual Wikipedia + web crawl | Native Thai processing; cross-lingual transfer potential | Less medical-specific; fine-tuning on Thai medical corpus recommended |
| **Thai-specific fine-tuned** | Thai medical literature + MIMS/RCPT corpus | Optimal Thai medical semantic capture; local terminology and abbreviations | Requires substantial training data; ongoing maintenance for vocabulary evolution |

**Recommended architecture**: **English content** (international guideline adaptations, primary research abstracts) → **PubMedBERT/BioBERT**; **Thai content** (RCPT primary documents, MIMS Thai-language sections) → **XLM-R fine-tuned on Thai medical corpus** or **parallel indexing with translation verification**.

#### 6.3.2 Chunk size optimization: 512 tokens with 128-token overlap

The **512-token chunk size with 128-token overlap** represents **empirical optimization** balancing:

| Factor | 512-Token Configuration | Alternative Considerations |
|--------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Semantic coherence** | Preserves complete algorithm steps or recommendation statements with context | Smaller chunks (256) for precise retrieval; larger (1024) for complex multi-step reasoning |
| **Embedding quality** | Within typical model maximum sequence lengths; avoids truncation artifacts | Some models support 1024+ tokens with quality trade-offs |
| **Retrieval precision** | Enables fine-grained matching to specific query intents | Overlap ensures boundary-spanning content remains retrievable |
| **Storage efficiency** | Moderate index size with acceptable redundancy | Overlap increases storage ~25% but improves boundary coverage |

**Adaptive chunking** for **algorithm-specific structures**: preserve **complete decision branches** (condition + action + alternative) even if exceeding nominal token limit; use **hierarchical chunking** with summary parent and detail children for complex multi-step pathways.

#### 6.3.3 Retrieval augmentation: Hybrid dense-sparse indexing for algorithm precision

| Index Type | Query Type Optimization | Implementation |
|------------|------------------------|----------------|
| **Dense (neural embedding)** | Conceptual similarity; paraphrased queries; symptom-to-disease mapping | FAISS/Annoy with cosine similarity; approximate nearest neighbors for scale |
| **Sparse (BM25/TF-IDF)** | Exact terminology matching; medication names; diagnostic criteria thresholds; numeric values | Inverted index with BM25 scoring; field-weighted for title/heading emphasis |
| **Metadata filtering** | Specialty restriction; evidence grade minimum; date range; patient population | Pre-filtered candidate set before similarity ranking |
| **Hybrid fusion** | Combined semantic + lexical relevance with learned weights | Linear combination or RRF (Reciprocal Rank Fusion); query-type adaptive weighting |

**Algorithm-specific retrieval enhancement**: **structured query parsing** to identify decision point type (diagnosis, treatment, monitoring); **pathway-aware ranking** that prioritizes sequential algorithm steps when prior context is known; and **contraindication surfacing** that retrieves precautionary content even for seemingly unrelated queries.

## 7. Implementation & Access Considerations

### 7.1 Licensing & Copyright Framework

#### 7.1.1 MIMS Thailand: Terms of use for educational/clinical decision support

MIMS Thailand operates under **commercial terms of service** that require careful review for RAG dataset construction and deployment . Key considerations include: **educational and clinical decision support use** is typically permitted for individual practitioners; **systematic extraction and derivative dataset creation** may require explicit licensing; **enterprise integration** in hospital information systems or commercial products necessitates contractual negotiation; and **attribution requirements** for MIMS content used in RAG outputs. **Recommended approach**: direct engagement with MIMS (Pte) Ltd for **authorized API access or data licensing agreement** that ensures sustainable, permissioned integration while respecting commercial interests.

#### 7.1.2 RCPT/Society guidelines: Open access policies with attribution requirements

RCPT and specialty society guidelines generally adopt **open access policies** that encourage widespread dissemination for quality improvement . Typical terms include: **free download and use** for educational and clinical purposes; **explicit attribution** in any derivative works or presentations; **non-commercial preference** with commercial use requiring notification or permission; and **prohibition of misrepresentation** or alteration of recommendation meaning. These terms are **readily compatible with academic and public sector RAG deployment**, with appropriate citation preservation in retrieval outputs.

#### 7.1.3 International adaptations: License compatibility for derivative works

International guideline adaptations present **complex license compatibility** when Thai modifications create derivative works from copyrighted source guidelines. **Best practices**: explicit documentation of adaptation methodology and divergence points; preservation of original source attribution; verification that machine learning model training on guideline content falls within permitted transformative use; and legal review for commercial deployment scenarios. **Creative Commons-licensed international guidelines** (increasingly common for WHO and some specialty society content) provide clearer permission frameworks.

## 8. Disease-Specific Algorithm Priorities for RAG Enrichment

### 8.1 High-Burden Non-Communicable Diseases

#### 8.1.1 Cardiovascular: Hypertension, Heart Failure, Dyslipidemia, CAD

**Priority rationale**: Cardiovascular diseases account for **approximately 30% of Thai mortality**, with substantial **preventable burden** through guideline-concordant care. **RAG enrichment focus**:

| Condition | Algorithm Enhancement Priority | Specific Content Targets |
|-----------|------------------------------|------------------------|
| **Hypertension** | Very High | Home/ambulatory BP monitoring integration; resistant hypertension evaluation; combination therapy selection with single-pill preference; age-stratified targets  |
| **Heart Failure** | Very High | Phenotype-specific therapy (HFrEF/HFmrEF/HFpEF); four-drug foundational regimen up-titration; device therapy candidacy; decompensation prevention  |
| **Dyslipidemia** | Very High | Thai CV Risk Score application; statin intensity by risk category; non-statin addition criteria; familial hypercholesterolemia identification  |
| **Coronary Artery Disease** | High | Acute coronary syndrome pathway differentiation; revascularization timing; secondary prevention intensity; chronic coronary syndrome follow-up  |

#### 8.1.2 Metabolic: Type 2 Diabetes, Obesity, Metabolic Syndrome

**Priority rationale**: Thailand's **diabetes prevalence (~10-12% of adults)** and **accelerating obesity epidemic** create extraordinary healthcare burden with **proven intervention effectiveness**. **RAG enrichment focus**: **glycemic target individualization** by age and comorbidity; **pharmacotherapy sequencing** with cardiovascular/renal outcome trial integration; **insulin intensification** with analog selection and monitoring; **complication screening** with retinal, renal, and cardiovascular surveillance protocols; and **weight management** with lifestyle, pharmacotherapy, and surgical options .

#### 8.1.3 Oncology: Breast, Lung, Colorectal, Liver, Cervical Cancer

**Priority rationale**: These **five sites represent majority of Thai cancer incidence and mortality**, with **rapidly evolving therapeutic landscape** requiring current guidance. **RAG enrichment focus**: **screening eligibility and interval optimization**; **staging-appropriate multimodality treatment sequencing**; **molecular profiling and targeted therapy selection**; **immunotherapy toxicity management**; **palliative care integration**; and **survivorship care planning** .

#### 8.1.4 Respiratory: COPD, Asthma, Lung Cancer screening

**Priority rationale**: **High prevalence, substantial symptom burden, and proven intervention underutilization** create quality improvement opportunity. **RAG enrichment focus**: **asthma control assessment with stepwise adjustment** ; **COPD diagnosis confirmation and exacerbation prevention**; **inhaler technique and adherence optimization**; **air pollution exacerbation management**; and **lung cancer screening eligibility with low-dose CT implementation** .

### 8.2 Priority Infectious Diseases

#### 8.2.1 Tropical/Neglected: Malaria, Dengue, Leptospirosis, Melioidosis

**Priority rationale**: These **region-endemic conditions** have **limited international guideline coverage** but **substantial Thai expertise and disease burden**. **RAG enrichment focus**: **species-differentiated and resistance-adapted malaria treatment** with artemisinin combination therapy optimization ; **dengue severity grading with fluid management** and warning sign recognition ; **leptospirosis early antibiotic therapy** with doxycycline or azithromycin; and **melioidosis prolonged eradication therapy** with Burkholderia pseudomallei-specific protocols.

#### 8.2.2 Emerging: COVID-19 variants, Influenza pandemic preparedness

**Priority rationale**: **Demonstrated pandemic impact and ongoing evolution** requires **rapidly updatable guidance infrastructure**. **RAG enrichment focus**: **variant-specific clinical characteristics and therapeutic adjustments**; **antiviral therapy selection by timing and risk factors** ; **vaccination strategy integration**; and **preparedness protocols for novel pathogen emergence** with scalable rapid guideline development.

#### 8.2.3 Antimicrobial Resistance: Stewardship protocols, Resistance surveillance

**Priority rationale**: **Thailand's substantial resistance burden** and **global health security imperative** demand **guideline-concordant prescribing**. **RAG enrichment focus**: **diagnostic stewardship with culture-directed therapy**; **empirical therapy selection by site and severity with local resistance data** ; **de-escalation and duration optimization**; and **infection prevention and control integration**.

### 8.3 Maternal-Child Health

#### 8.3.1 Obstetric emergencies: Pre-eclampsia, Eclampsia, Postpartum hemorrhage

**Priority rationale**: **Time-critical, high-mortality conditions** where **protocol-guided management improves outcomes**. **RAG enrichment focus**: **severe feature recognition with magnesium sulfate neuroprotection** ; **blood pressure emergency management** with appropriate antihypertensive selection; **eclampsia seizure control and delivery timing**; and **postpartum hemorrhage escalating intervention** with uterotonics, tamponade, and surgical options.

#### 8.3.2 Neonatal care: Birth asphyxia, Sepsis, Jaundice management

**Priority rationale**: **Early intervention in critical window** with **lifetime impact**. **RAG enrichment focus**: **resuscitation with positive pressure ventilation and chest compression protocols**; **therapeutic hypothermia for moderate-severe hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy**; **early-onset sepsis risk stratification and empirical antibiotics**; and **hyperbilirubinemia risk assessment with phototherapy and exchange transfusion thresholds** .

#### 8.3.3 Pediatric development: Growth monitoring, Vaccination schedules, Developmental delays

**Priority rationale**: **Preventive and early intervention opportunities** with **substantial lifetime health impact**. **RAG enrichment focus**: **growth velocity assessment with nutritional and endocrine evaluation triggers**; **national immunization schedule with catch-up protocols**; **developmental milestone surveillance with early intervention referral**; and **common pediatric infections with age-appropriate antimicrobial selection**.

### 8.4 Mental Health & Neurology

#### 8.4.1 Common disorders: Depression, Anxiety, Insomnia screening and management

**Priority rationale**: **High prevalence, substantial disability, and frequently suboptimal recognition/treatment** in primary care. **RAG enrichment focus**: **screening tool integration (PHQ-9, GAD-7, ISI)** with appropriate cutoff interpretation; **stepped-care treatment with pharmacotherapy and psychotherapy coordination** ; **first-line agent selection with side effect counseling**; and **referral triggers for specialist involvement**.

#### 8.4.2 Severe mental illness: Schizophrenia, Bipolar disorder long-term care

**Priority rationale**: **Chronic conditions requiring sustained engagement** with **medication adherence challenges**. **RAG enrichment focus**: **antipsychotic selection with metabolic monitoring**; **long-acting injectable therapy for adherence optimization**; **mood stabilizer optimization with level monitoring**; **relapse prevention and early intervention**; and **family involvement and psychosocial support integration**.

#### 8.4.3 Neurological emergencies: Stroke thrombolysis, Status epilepticus, Meningitis

**Priority rationale**: **Time-critical conditions with outcome-dependent rapid intervention**. **RAG enrichment focus**: **thrombolysis eligibility with time-window and contraindication assessment** ; **thrombectomy candidacy and transfer protocols**; **status epilepticus benzodiazepine escalation and anesthetic management**; and **meningitis empirical antibiotics by age and immune status with rapid diagnostic testing integration**.

This consistency enables: **unified RAG construction methodologies** adaptable across markets; **healthcare professional mobility** with familiar interface patterns; and **regional content harmonization** where appropriate.

| Dimension | Thai Localization | Philippine Localization | Implication for RAG |

### 9.2 RCPT vs. International Society Guidelines

#### 9.2.1 Evidence base: Local population data vs. global clinical trials

| Aspect | RCPT/Thai Society Guidelines | International Society Guidelines | Integration Strategy |

| **Safety profile** | Thai adverse event surveillance; herbal medicine interaction data | Trial safety with post-marketing surveillance | Enhanced pharmacovigilance; traditional medicine integration |

#### 9.2.2 Resource adaptation: Cost-effectiveness for Thai healthcare system

**RCPT guidelines explicitly address cost-effectiveness** through: **health technology assessment integration** with HITAP evaluation for expensive interventions; **generic prioritization** where bioequivalence established; **stepped-care protocols** that reserve expensive options for refractory cases; and **implementation feasibility assessment** for resource-limited settings. **International guidelines** increasingly incorporate cost considerations but may assume **resource availability inconsistent with Thai practice**.

#### 9.2.3 Implementation feasibility: Primary care vs. tertiary center protocols

**RCPT guideline strength**: **stratified recommendations by care setting capability**, with explicit **primary care-appropriate pathways** and **clear specialist referral triggers**. This **pragmatic approach** contrasts with **international guidelines that may assume specialist-managed care** or provide **less explicit primary care guidance**.
